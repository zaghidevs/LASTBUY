LAST documentation

How races run, how the prize is paid, and how to check it yourself.

Overview

LAST is a pump.fun coin, $LAST, that runs a race that never stops. The coin's creator fees build up a prize. Each qualifying buy makes the buyer the leader and resets a 10-minute clock. When the clock reaches zero, the leader wins the prize and a new race starts.

Nobody runs the races by hand. The LAST race bot watches every $LAST trade on Solana, keeps the clock, collects the creator fees into the prize wallet, and sends each winner their prize automatically.

How a race works

1
Fees fill the prize

Every trade on the coin pays a creator fee. For $LAST, that fee goes to the prize wallet.

2
Buy to take the lead

A buy of at least 0.1 SOL makes you the leader and sets the clock back to 10:00.

3
The flag drops

If 10 minutes pass with no qualifying buy, the race ends. The leader at that moment is the winner.

4
The prize is paid

90% of the prize goes to the winner's wallet. The other 10% becomes the starting prize for the next race.

Races run back to back for as long as $LAST trades. After a race is paid, the next race starts with the next qualifying buy.

Taking the lead

Only buys count, from anywhere: pump.fun, trading bots, or any app that trades $LAST. A buy takes the lead when it spends at least 0.1 SOL in a single transaction. Smaller buys still trade normally and still add creator fees to the prize, but they don't change the leader or the clock.

Sells

Selling never affects the race. You stay the leader even if you sell your tokens after buying. The race rewards the last buyer, not the biggest holder.

Buys in the same block

Solana orders transactions inside each block. When two qualifying buys land in the same block, the one ordered later becomes the leader.

Buying right at zero

The clock uses on-chain block time. A buy only counts for a race if its block time is before the clock reaches zero. A buy that lands after zero starts the next race instead.

The prize

The prize is the SOL balance of the prize wallet, which is also the creator of $LAST. Its address is on the home page, and you can check its balance and every transaction on Solscan at any time.

How payout happens

When the clock hits zero, the bot waits 20 seconds to catch any buys that landed just before the deadline. Then it collects the latest creator fees and sends 90% of the prize wallet's balance straight to the winner's wallet. There's nothing to claim.

Every payout is listed in Results on the home page with a link to its transaction.

Rules

These are the rules the race bot runs on.

Race clock10:00
Minimum buy to lead0.1 SOL
SellsNo effect
Winner's share90%
Carried to next race10%
Longest race24 hours
After 24 hoursClock resets to 60s instead of 10:00

Why there's a 24-hour limit

Without a limit, a busy coin could keep one race going for days while the prize grows. After 24 hours, each buy only resets the clock to 60 seconds, so the race ends soon after trading slows down.

You don't have to trust the site's numbers. Everything can be checked on-chain.

To check a race yourself, look at the last $LAST buy of 0.1 SOL or more before the clock ran out, then find the payout to that wallet from the prize wallet.

Who holds the prize walletThe prize wallet is run by the LAST race bot, and the LAST team holds its key. The bot has no way to send the prize anywhere except to race winners, and every transaction from the wallet is public.

Risks

Read this before you buyMemecoins are highly volatile. You can lose everything you put in, even while trying to win a race.
  • Being outbid. Buying to take the lead doesn't mean you'll win. Someone can buy after you in the last seconds, and most leaders don't win.
  • Token price. A winning buy still leaves you holding tokens, which can drop in value faster than the prize makes up for.
  • Bots. Automated traders can watch the clock and buy in the final seconds. The minimum buy and the 24-hour limit reduce this but don't stop it.
  • Bot downtime. If the race bot goes offline, payouts are delayed until it's back. Buys made while it's offline still count, because it reads them from the chain when it restarts.
  • Network issues. If Solana is congested, your buy may land late or fail.
  • Custody. The prize is held in a wallet controlled by the LAST team's bot, not a smart contract.
  • Your location. Prize mechanics may be restricted where you live. You're responsible for following your local laws.

FAQ

Where do I buy?

Anywhere $LAST trades. The Take the lead button opens it on pump.fun, but buys from trading bots and other apps count too.

Do I have to hold the coin to win?

No. The winner is the wallet that made the last qualifying buy. What you do with the tokens afterwards doesn't matter.

What happens if nobody buys at all?

The clock only starts with the first qualifying buy. Until then, the prize waits in the wallet.

How do I know the payouts are real?

Every payout links to its Solana transaction. You can also open the prize wallet on Solscan and see every transfer it has ever made.

Does LAST charge a fee?

LAST takes nothing from prizes. Normal pump.fun trading fees and Solana network fees still apply.

Can I win more than once?

Yes. There's no limit on how many races a wallet can win.

How fast is the prize paid?

Usually within a minute of the clock hitting zero, including the 20-second wait for late buys.
